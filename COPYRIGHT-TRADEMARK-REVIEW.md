# Ironcraft copyright and trademark review

Review date: 2026-09-12  
Scope: the current `minecraft.html` game, its user interface, names, visual assets, gameplay presentation, and public marketing copy.

This is a practical product review, not a legal opinion or a guarantee. A qualified intellectual-property lawyer should review a renamed and redesigned build before monetization or a larger launch.

## Finding

The general idea of a block-based, procedurally generated survival and building game is not itself owned by Microsoft or Mojang. Game ideas, titles, and methods of play are generally outside copyright protection, although the original expression of those ideas can be protected.

The current Ironcraft build presents a high combined copyright and trademark risk because it reproduces a large collection of Minecraft-specific expression and identifiers in the same product category. The concern is the combined commercial impression, not merely the use of cubes.

## Highest-risk elements

| Current element | Why it is too close | Original replacement direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Ironcraft** name | It follows the same material-plus-“craft” naming pattern and is used for a highly similar voxel survival game. Similar sound, appearance, meaning, and commercial impression can matter in a trademark analysis. | Choose a cleared name that avoids “mine,” “craft,” “block,” Mojang terms, and similar logos. Run a professional trademark search before committing to it. |
| Title and world-selection screens | The previous dirt-tile backdrop, huge block title, angled yellow splash, stacked beveled gray buttons, “Singleplayer,” “Multiplayer,” “Options...,” “Select World,” and “Play Selected World” closely tracked Minecraft’s screen composition and wording. | Use the Iron Games industrial/expedition visual system, different layout, typography, animation, button shapes, colors, and vocabulary. An initial reskin and wording change has been made, but every menu should receive the same treatment. |
| HUD | Pixel hearts, hunger drumsticks, armor icons, green experience bar, crosshair, and nine-slot hotbar closely reproduced a distinctive combination. | Use labeled meters, a different inventory shape and position, original iconography, and an original selection treatment. The in-game status row has now been changed to labeled health, energy, armor, and air meters; the inventory still needs a full redesign. |
| Creeper and creeper-face texture | The green tall creature, square dark face, and explosive behavior are exceptionally recognizable Minecraft expression. | Remove it. Create a creature with a different silhouette, palette, face, movement, sound, and attack behavior. |
| Minecraft-specific mobs | Enderman, Warden, Villager, Piglin, Blaze, Shulker, Guardian, Pillager, Evoker, Vindicator, Wither, and Ender Dragon use Minecraft-specific names and recognizable roles or forms. | Replace with original factions and creatures. Write a short silhouette, movement, habitat, behavior, and sound brief for each before making art. |
| Minecraft-specific realms and structures | Nether, End, Overworld, trial chambers, ancient cities, strongholds, sculk areas, End portals, and Nether portals recreate Minecraft’s world structure. | Build an original cosmology and progression. Example directions include a buried heat realm, a wind-carved sky realm, and a storm frontier, each with unique travel rules rather than copied portals. |
| Blocks and items | Names and designs such as crafting table, netherrack, soul sand, redstone ore, end stone, end portal frame, enchanting table, elytra, ender pearl, blaze rod, eye of ender, totem of undying, nether star, shulker box, and dragon egg are strongly associated with Minecraft. | Rename and redesign the full catalog. Generic materials such as wood, stone, iron, sand, water, and glass may remain if their art is original. |
| Recipes, enchantments, and progression | The current build duplicates Minecraft’s recipes, villager professions and levels, enchantment names and rules, boss recipes, portal sequence, and “four shrieks wake the Warden” progression. | Design a new recipe grammar, upgrade system, non-villager economy, realm access rules, bosses, and endgame loop. |
| Textures and character model | Even procedurally generated files can infringe when the resulting art intentionally recreates protected textures, faces, or character proportions. The current Steve-like preview and several mob faces are particularly close. | Adopt a documented art system: a different resolution, non-square facial construction, distinct body proportions, new palette, new surface treatment, and original source sketches. |
| Public filename and SEO | The `minecraft.html` route and former “Minecraft-style” metadata reinforce an association with the official product. | After the final new name is selected, use a new route and redirect the old route. Minecraft references have been removed from public metadata in the interim. |

## Elements that can remain with original expression

- A world made from voxels or modular cells
- Procedural terrain generation
- Building, digging, collecting, crafting, farming, survival, and day/night systems as broad ideas
- Generic tools and materials such as a hammer, axe, shovel, wood, stone, iron, food, and water
- Original tornadoes, extreme weather, expedition objectives, cooperative building, and survival challenges
- Generic fantasy creatures such as skeletons or zombies when their art, behavior, naming, sound, and lore are independently designed

## Recommended redesign sequence

1. Select a new name through a proper clearance search. Do not use a candidate publicly until the search is complete.
2. Write a one-page original-world brief: setting, player role, three regions, resource loop, enemies, allies, travel system, and end goal.
3. Replace the Creeper and all Minecraft-specific mobs, bosses, realms, structures, items, recipes, enchantments, and terminology.
4. Redesign every menu, inventory screen, icon, meter, tooltip, and character model from an Iron Games design system.
5. Create art from original sketches and prompts that describe the desired materials and mood without naming Minecraft or asking for a Minecraft-like result.
6. Keep an asset provenance log containing the creator, date, source file, prompt or sketch, and license for every visual and sound.
7. Search code, visible text, metadata, filenames, documentation, and screenshots for remaining Mojang identifiers.
8. Have an intellectual-property lawyer review the final name, logo, screenshots, and build before monetization or a broad promotional campaign.

## Interim risk reduction already applied

- Removed “Minecraft-style” and Minecraft-specific feature names from public page metadata.
- Replaced the closest title-menu background, button styling, splash phrases, and menu wording with an Iron Games visual direction.
- Replaced the copied heart, hunger, armor, and air HUD presentation with labeled meters.
- Added the Minecraft Usage Guidelines disclaimer to the title screen.

These changes reduce the immediate presentation risk. They do not resolve the copied mobs, objects, progression, realms, recipes, enchantments, interfaces, or the name itself.
