# Ironcraft copyright and trademark review — version 2

Review date: 2026-09-12  
Scope: the current published `minecraft.html` build, including menus, HUD, inventory interfaces, generated art, creatures, blocks, items, recipes, progression, names, metadata, filenames, and public pages.

This is a practical product-risk review, not a legal opinion or a guarantee. Only a court can decide infringement, and an intellectual-property lawyer should review the final name and redesigned build before monetization or a larger launch.

## Direct answer

**The current build is likely over the practical safety line for a standalone game. Overall risk remains HIGH.**

The voxel format, procedural terrain, collecting, building, crafting, and survival ideas may be used in an original game. The problem is the volume and coordination of Minecraft-specific expression in Ironcraft: recognizable creatures and faces, named realms, blocks, items, interface arrangements, recipes, enchantments, bosses, structures, and progression all appear together in the same type of game.

The recent redesign materially lowered the risk of the title screen and status HUD. It did not clear the underlying game content. A targeted scan found 39 high-signal Minecraft terms appearing 627 times in `minecraft.html`; this count is evidence of the scope of the overlap, not a legal test.

## Copyright analysis

The U.S. Copyright Office says game ideas, game names, and methods of play are not protected by copyright, while literary and pictorial expression can be. That means the team may build an original voxel survival game, but should not recreate Minecraft's protected art, characters, written content, or distinctive audiovisual presentation.

The strongest copyright concerns are:

- **Creeper:** green mottled texture; tall four-legged/block-bodied silhouette; square black face; silent approach and explosion. The name, face, silhouette, palette, and behavior together are exceptionally recognizable.
- **Enderman:** very tall black humanoid; long limbs; purple eyes; teleporting and block interaction. The combined character treatment is too close.
- **Warden and sculk system:** dark teal body, light rib/horn motifs, vibration hunting, sonic attack, sculk sensors/shriekers, and the awakening sequence. This is a highly specific modern Minecraft content set.
- **Wither:** floating three-headed dark boss, wither skull projectiles, summoning recipe, Nether Star drop, and beacon payoff.
- **Ender Dragon progression:** eyes of ender locate a stronghold; portal frames lead to the End; crystals heal the dragon; the player receives a dragon egg and later accesses End cities, shulkers, and elytra.
- **Nether content:** netherrack, soul sand, glowstone, nether quartz, ancient debris, Nether portals, fortresses, Blazes, Piglins, Wither Skeletons, and the same resource/progression roles.
- **Villager system:** recognizable face and proportions, professions, tiered trades, emerald economy, trade exhaustion, and restocking.
- **Interface expression:** the inventory/crafting/furnace/chest/enchanting screens retain Minecraft-like colors, slot bevels, grid geometry, arrows, and relationships even after the outer menu redesign.

Colors and cubes alone are weak evidence. Risk comes from combinations such as a mottled green palette plus the Creeper face, silhouette, name, and explosion, or a gray beveled panel plus the same slot geometry, crafting relationships, and item catalog.

## Trademark and source-confusion analysis

The USPTO explains that marks do not have to be identical; similarity in sound, appearance, meaning, or overall commercial impression can matter, especially for related goods. `Ironcraft` and `Minecraft` are both used for voxel survival games and share the “material + craft” construction. That alone does not establish infringement, but the identical market and extensive Minecraft-specific content substantially increase the source-confusion concern.

The following make the association stronger:

- The public filename and canonical URL use `minecraft.html`.
- The game contains the word Minecraft in the disclaimer and code, plus hundreds of Minecraft-specific identifiers.
- The content experience immediately presents Minecraft creatures, realms, items, recipes, enchanting, and endgame structure.
- Many public activity pages repeat the `Ironcraft` brand, increasing the cost of a later rename.

A disclaimer helps disclose the lack of affiliation, but it does not erase copied expression or automatically prevent confusion.

## Minecraft Usage Guidelines assessment

The current disclaimer follows Mojang's suggested format and should remain while any Minecraft reference remains. The guidelines also define Minecraft's brand broadly to include names, logos, fonts, textures, and other distinctive characteristics, and define assets to include graphics, textures, models, sounds, code, screenshots, and other game material.

Mojang says public sharing counts as commercial use under its guidelines even when no money is charged. The guidelines permit certain fan activity and descriptive secondary use; they should not be treated as a license to publish a standalone browser replacement that recreates Minecraft's content system. On the present facts, guideline compliance should be treated as **not established and high risk**.

## Clean-room redesign plan

No process can promise “100% original” or zero legal risk. The strongest practical approach is a documented clean-room redesign:

1. **Freeze new Minecraft-derived content.** Add no more official names, recipes, creatures, textures, realms, structures, enchantments, or update features.
2. **Choose a new world concept first.** Define the player's role, resource loop, three regions, travel system, allies, enemies, crafting logic, and end goal without looking at Minecraft references during design.
3. **Choose a cleared name.** Avoid “mine,” “craft,” and Mojang identifiers in the candidate set. Search federal registrations, state records, common-law use, domains, app stores, game platforms, and social handles; then have counsel assess the finalists.
4. **Create an art bible.** Use a distinct palette, texture resolution and surface treatment, body proportions, facial grammar, animation style, icon shapes, typography, sound palette, and interface geometry. Preserve sketches and dated drafts.
5. **Replace the content catalog by function.** For each current item or creature, write its gameplay job in generic terms, then invent a different name, silhouette, habitat, behavior, sound, reward, and counterplay. Do not make one-to-one cosmetic renames.
6. **Invent a new progression graph.** Use different gates, currencies, crafting relationships, travel mechanics, bosses, and win condition. Avoid the Overworld/Nether/End sequence and Minecraft's recipe tree.
7. **Rebuild every utility interface.** Use a different inventory organization, slot shape, crafting method, equipment layout, upgrade screen, trade model, and feedback system.
8. **Migrate branding and URLs.** Replace `Ironcraft`, rename `minecraft.html`, update canonical tags, structured data, guide/activity pages, sitemap entries, media filenames, and redirects. Keep the old URL only as a neutral redirect.
9. **Keep a provenance ledger.** Record creator, date, brief, sketches, prompts, source files, third-party licenses, and approvals for every asset and sound.
10. **Run a release audit.** Search visible text, source, comments, IDs, filenames, metadata, guides, screenshots, and videos for Mojang identifiers; compare the final build side by side; then obtain counsel review.

## Changes already made and their effect

- Replaced the dirt-block title backdrop and gray Minecraft-like menu with an Iron Games dark grid and gold/ember treatment.
- Replaced “Singleplayer,” “Play Selected World,” and related wording with “Solo Worlds,” “Your Expeditions,” and original navigation language.
- Replaced pixel heart, hunger, armor, and air icon rows with labeled meters.
- Removed “Minecraft-style” and Minecraft feature keywords from public marketing metadata.
- Added the recommended non-affiliation disclaimer.

These were worthwhile risk reductions. The title screen is no longer the largest issue. The content catalog and internal gameplay systems now deserve nearly all redesign effort.
