What this mission is about

Iron Battle’s changing supply, unit counts and objectives create numbers with context. A player can measure how quickly resources change, compare the cost of plans and estimate distances on the map. Define the question first so play produces usable data.

Set up the mission

Prepare a table with time, supply, friendly units and objectives held. Record at regular intervals such as every two minutes rather than only when something dramatic happens. Iron Battle is a real-time strategy game about units, objectives, supply and support choices.

Challenge steps

Record starting values and three timed checkpoints.

Calculate supply gained or spent between checkpoints.

Find the percentage of the map objectives held at each checkpoint.

Estimate the map distance between headquarters and two objectives.

Compare cost per surviving unit for two recruitment choices.

Graph one value across the full operation.

Talk it through

A short explanation turns an exciting moment into a chance to organize evidence and ideas. Ask one or two questions instead of turning the whole session into a quiz.

  • Which value changed steadily and which changed suddenly?
  • Does holding more objectives appear alongside faster supply growth?
  • What could make the apparent relationship misleading?

Level it up

Repeat the table on another operation. Normalize one measure, such as supply per minute, so operations of different lengths can be compared.

A note for parents and homeschool adults

Set the session length and learning goal before play, then leave room for the child’s own solution. Treat the game as a prompt for practice and questions. Verify history and science claims with reliable outside sources. Ironcraft public multiplayer can include other players and chat, so supervise online play and use a private room for a known group.
